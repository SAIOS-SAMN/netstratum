# 76 — A story that emerges: the reference narrative

Operator (2026-10-13): one narrative for one universe, as an example of how
such stories emerge. It is not a script. Nothing below is written into the
universe; each element must come out of the physics, and the test is that a
story like this can be read off a universe's chronicle after the fact.

## The narrative

A big bang happened in a section of the void and made a singularity, where
the quantum bounce distributed the elements put into it through the universe.
The first stars and planets were born of this.

The innermost ring past the accretion disc filled with a certain kind of
planet, and the first civilizations were born there: the ancients. They were
inundated with temporal waves at a rate the outer rim cannot imagine, as a
new single-world civilization on a linear timeline. So the ancients bore the
progenitors, to go out among the stars and create wormholes inside the
galaxy: superhighways for resource transport. The ancients like to stay on
their home planets, except for visits to very specific kinds of planet that
mimic their extreme gravity and temporal resonance.

Toward the outer rim, civilizations were born of the distribution of
elements. Some grew, some fell. Those that grew looked up at the stars with
wonder, built science, overcame their single-planet dramas and
conflicts, built stardocks and ships, and learned to walk among the stars.
Some systems already had wormholes from the progenitor network; some did not.
Those that did not were tied to the science and resources they gained on
their own. They could not go as far across the galaxy as with a local
wormhole, but some of them did transit, and others faltered after colonizing
several planets, leaving archaeology.

As the captains became sovereigns in their own right, the progenitors were out
designing superhighways. Where these met from opposing ends of the temporal
lineage, realms were created, where civilizations learned to work together,
or to fight wars together or against each other. Some civilizations were
dark-aligned, some light, and some both. The progenitors themselves went to
war once and were almost all wiped out, but that is one story on one
timeline. The realms grew, and as different realms met, they found that
different realms have different rules, and that is fine. Councils of the
realms met to discuss how their realms would interact. Sometimes they
devolved into war; sometimes there were eons of prosperity in resource
distribution.

## The rule: physics defines, the story emerges

The physics is defined; the story is what comes out of it. No element of the
story is a feature, a rule or a milestone in the code. For each element the
question is only: does the physics make it possible, and unscripted? Where the
physics is missing, the physics is what gets defined, never the element.

What to define next comes from the undefined rows, as physics:
- time varying with distance to a core;
- whether a gate pays back;

- contested mass.

Each must be derived and justified on its own terms, not by this story.

## The test

When a universe has run, its chronicle is read for this story's shape, never
for this story:
- who arose first and where;
- which networks spread and where they met;
- which realms formed, warred and prospered;
- which civilizations fell and what they left.

A universe that tells a different story has passed the same test. A story
written into the code fails it.
