## 1. The turning

**From the motion law** (`ring`, docs/83): each ring a place receives turns
it by its deficit `δ = H/(R + H)` of a turn, in the plane it turns in. A
turning is therefore a plane and an exact rational angle, `δ × rings`
turns; never a cosine.

**From the layer ruling** (2026-10-18: "the motion and rotation of each
shell is orthogonal to the layer underneath"): each shell turns in a plane
orthogonal to the one beneath it. On `S^n` (n + 1 ambient directions) at
most `⌊(n + 1)/2⌋` planes can be COMPLETELY orthogonal, sharing no
direction, and rotations in completely orthogonal planes COMMUTE. Their
products are the sphere's maximal torus.

**So** a body's turning is one exact angle per plane, composing by adding
the angles plane by plane, in any order, exactly. No rotor components are
stored (Niven: the cosine and sine of a rational-degree angle are
irrational), and no ordered list of runs grows, because commuting factors
in one plane fold. The rotor is formed only to read a turning in another
frame (§3).

Where two turnings DO share a direction (not completely orthogonal), they
do not commute, and the exact object is the ordered runs `(plane, angle)`,
folding within a plane (with xylarium). The layer ruling keeps that to the
seams between tiers.

**Not a flattening**: the planes are independent dimensions of the sphere's
rotations, and the list of angles is their maximal torus, complete for
motions in orthogonal planes.

**Finding (owed a ruling):** netstratum turns every tier in TWO planes
(`flows::PLANES`, the orbit's and the circulation's). The tiers' spheres
offer `⌊(n + 1)/2⌋`: the atom's S^11 six, a system's S^60 thirty, a
galaxy's S^360 one hundred and eighty. Two planes on S^360 is a restriction
of dimensional capacity. How the further planes are filled (each shell's
orthogonal plane filling the next) is the operators' to rule.

## 2. The round

**Its centre** is the body's place, which the enclosure law already holds
as exact angles (`enclosure::place`: its polar share of a half turn from
the core, and its phase in each plane). It is a turning from the frame,
§1, so the centre is never a coordinate.

**Its shape.** Closeness on a circle is the ruled linear coherence,
`in_step(Δ) = 1 − 2|Δ|` (docs/79: linear, exact, in place of `(1 + cos)/2`).
On the sphere a body's turning is shared among its planes: the weight
`w_k` of plane k is its share of the body's spin there (the split
`ℓ_o : ℓ_c` today), `Σ w_k = 1`, held as shares, never as squared radii.
A place's closeness to the centre is

    C = Σ_k w_k · in_step(Δ_k)

the same rule in every direction, so the region `C ≥ c` is ROUND: a cap,
not a box. It is the sphere's own closeness on its maximal torus
(`cos d = Σ w_k cos Δ_k`, with `w_k` the point's share in plane k) under the
ruled linear measure. It is exact over ℚ, and nothing is squared. (The
firmament's repulsion and the wind read closeness as a PRODUCT over planes,
weighing every plane alike and multiplying, which is not the sphere's
closeness; they move to this form.)

**Its extent** is the body's own SPREAD. Agitation `a` is the share of the
body's turning absorbed and not re-locked (`a = (p − δ)/p` at a cut; the
kick's heat `p·δ·(1 − |k|)`, its out-of-step share). An out-of-step share
`a` is a closeness `1 − a`, so the body's cap is

    { places with C ≥ 1 − a },  half-angle a/2 of a turn (180·a degrees)

In reach, the same share is the spread `R(1 ± a)`, a DILATION by the same
share. Rotation and dilation both bounded by the one share `a` make the
round conformal (containment in degrees, dilation per tier).

**Narrowing is cooling**: `a` falls by the two channels compounding
(`compound`, the open frames' `e` and the vacuum's `δ`), and the cap
contracts with it. A decision that needs a narrower cap than the body has
is answered by the body's state, never by refining a number. **Widening is
heat**, or a residue handed down (§4).

## 3. The reading

An observer receives a body by its light rings and resolves it with ITS
OWN enclosure's places: its tier's protractor in each plane, 60 a turn on
S^60, 360 on S^360. Those are the widths the merge marks used, and they
move from the state into the reading.

Its MARK-CAPS are the caps of its own sphere about its marks, a cap of
half a place about each, closeness read in the observer's own weights. A
READING is the set of the observer's mark-caps the body's cap meets: one
when the body is narrower than the observer's place, several when it is
wider. The observer reads a region, never a point. The mark centres and
the closeness are rational, so the reading is exact. It is never written
back into the body's state.

Across tiers the observer's mark-caps and the body's cap meet through the
throats on the route between them (`enclosure::route`), each tier's
dilation applied in turn (owed: the conformal map per tier, written out).

## 5. How the further planes fill

**The template is the atom's GRAMMAR of orientations**, read on each
tier's own sphere by that tier's own mechanism (operator 2026-10-16: "not
one law every tier, too linear; one grammar, coupled tiers, each tier's own
mechanism"). Electrons fill by tightness; bodies fill by what their tier's
law gathers. `cfe::almanac::atom` already fills S^11 by this grammar: "twelve dimensions,
six turning planes, no axis". Each electron takes the TIGHTEST open
winding, and within a shape takes a FREE orientation before it pairs. A
shape l offers the degree-l harmonics of the sphere, `C(l + n, n) −
C(l + n − 2, n)` orientations. The same law, read on each tier's own
sphere by that tier's own mechanism, fills its planes.

**The planes and the axis come from the sphere's parity.** S^n lies in
n + 1 directions. These pair into `⌊(n + 1)/2⌋` completely orthogonal
planes, and when n + 1 is odd one direction is left over that no plane
turns: an AXIS.

| Tier | Sphere | Planes | Axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| atom | S^11 | 6 | none |
| star system | S^60 | 30 | one |
| galaxy (and the universe) | S^360 | 180 | one |

The atom's own text ("six turning planes, no axis") is the first row. The
axis is the THROAT. On an even sphere (S^60, S^360) every continuous
turning has a point where it stops (Poincaré–Hopf: a sphere of even
dimension carries no turning that never vanishes), and that point is the
core each enclosure hangs on (`enclosure`: "the core is the THROAT").
An odd sphere (S^11) carries a turning that never stops, the Hopf fibre,
which the atom reads as spin, so the atom is closed: no throat, no core
point. One grammar, read by parity: enclosures with throats are the even
spheres, and the atom is the odd one.

**The first shape's orientations are the planes.** On S^n the degree-1
harmonics are the n + 1 directions. A turning in plane k is the complex
direction `x_{2k−1} + i·x_{2k}` or its conjugate, the two SENSES of that
plane. So shape 1 offers, on S^60, 30 planes × 2 senses + 1 axis = 61
orientations, and on S^360, 180 × 2 + 1 = 361. On S^11 it is 6 × 2 = 12,
the atom's count.

**The fill is read, never stated** (design laws: no fill orders; the
atom: "crossings between shells are read, never stated"). A body joining an
enclosure (a world about its star, a star about its galaxy's core) takes
the orientation its tier's mechanism reads as tightest, by the ring-density
law already used for both, and a FREE orientation before a counter-sense
(the atom's own rule). What follows from those two alone:

- while planes stand free, bodies that read alike take planes of their
  own, completely orthogonal to every plane already turning: the layer
  ruling ("each shell orthogonal to the layer underneath"), orthogonal to
  every layer underneath as capacity allows;
- a plane's other sense, counter-turning, is taken only where no free
  orientation reads tighter (the atom's pairing);
- the AXIS is an orientation like any other, taken where the reading puts
  it. A body there does not orbit: it moves along the throat, the axial
  leg the jets already run (`flows`), now an orientation and not an added
  path;
- the next shape (l = 2, eccentric windings) and the next shell are taken
  wherever they read tighter than what is left, as the atom's crossings
  are.

No step of this is an order written into the code: which orientation a
body takes is a reading of its tier's law at the moment it joins.

An even sphere has no Hopf fibre, so it has no spin doubling: each
orientation holds ONE body, where the atom's holds a pair.

**What follows.**

- **Bodies in different planes never meet.** Two great circles in
  completely orthogonal planes share no point, so co-orbital bodies in
  different planes pass through each other's reach without an encounter.
  The encounter law acts within a plane: counter-sensed bodies in one plane
  meet twice a turn, and bodies in one sense meet by overtaking. Bodies of
  planes that share a direction meet at the seams. The crowded orbits read
  before (period ratios near 1.000) are bodies the code forced into two
  planes.
- **A body's weights.** A body in one plane has its whole turning there,
  `w = 1` in that plane and 0 elsewhere, so its closeness C (§2) to a body
  in another plane is 0 until its residue or a kick gives it weight there.
- **Capacity is never restricted.** Nothing caps the planes at two. A
  system with fewer bodies than planes leaves planes empty: unoccupied,
  never removed.

**Owed.**

- At the star and galaxy tiers, which reach band is shell n, and how a
  shape's eccentricity reads on an orbit. The atom reads its shells from

- How the existing two planes (orbit and circulation) map to the plane

## 6. The shell bands at the star and galaxy tiers (derived; one tension found)

**The grammar** (the atom): a SHELL is the windings that close with whole
count n. **The tier's own mechanism** (the motion law, `ring`): a body at
reach R about the holding H inside it turns `δ = H/(R + H)` a ring, so one
turn takes `P = 1 + R/H` received rings. Rings are received WHOLE (each
clock counts them with `ring::whole`), so a turn is complete at the first
whole count at or past P. A body's shell is therefore READ from its reach:

    n = ⌈P⌉ = ⌈1 + R/H(<R)⌉,    shell n is the band (n − 2)·H < R ≤ (n − 1)·H

with H the holding enclosed at that reach (the enclosed law, `flows::Enclosed`).
It is nested by construction, as the atom's: the bands widen as the matter
inside them grows. It is linear, with no squares and no stated order.

What it reads, checked against laws already built:

- **Shell 1 is the centre** (R = 0). It holds only shape 0, the round
  winding, as the atom's shell 1 does: the heart, or the galactic core.
- **Shell 2 ends at the throat**, R = H, where δ = ½: the throat condition
  the heart forms at (`formation::ignition_share`: w = m, deficit ½) and the
  rim law's deficit ½. So the throat is the outer edge of shell 2, read,
  never placed.
- **Worlds and stars about a core stand from shell 3 outward.**

**The tension (owed to the operators before any build).** §5 took shape 1's
orientations as the planes. In the atom, a CIRCULAR winding of shell n has
the greatest shape, l = n − 1 (axes `(l + 1)/n = 1`), and its orientations
are the degree-(n − 1) harmonics: on S^60, 61 for n = 2 but 1,890 for
n = 3, and more beyond. A circular orbit is, geometrically, a turning in ONE
plane, and at most `⌊(n + 1)/2⌋` planes are completely orthogonal. So the
grammar's room for a shell (the harmonic count) and the turning's room (the
planes) agree only at shell 2. Two readings stand:

1. **Planes govern bodies.** A body's turning is a rotation in one plane.
   Bodies that do not meet need completely orthogonal planes, so a shell
   holds at most P non-meeting bodies (plus the counter-senses and the
   axis). The harmonic count is the atom's own mechanism and does not carry
   up. §5 stands as written; shells beyond 2 add room only through
   eccentric shapes and counter-senses.
2. **The harmonic count governs bodies too.** A shell's room at every tier
   is its harmonic count. Bodies of one shell then share planes, and the
   encounter law decides which of them meet. §5's "bodies in different
   planes never meet" stays true, but most bodies of a shell do not have a
   plane to themselves.

Reading 1 keeps the turning's geometry, and reading 2 keeps the grammar's
count. Choosing between them is a ruling, not a derivation: it decides
whether the atom's harmonic count is its own mechanism or the grammar.

### 6a. Which reading: derived (operator 2026-10-20: "derive which reading is correct")

Neither reading holds alone. Which one holds is READ from the occupant's
own round (§2), and the threshold falls where a law already built puts it.

**What an orientation counts.** The atom calls its orientations "exact
and topological": the degree-l harmonics are the sphere's own standing
patterns. Two harmonics of the same degree overlap everywhere on the sphere
and still never interfere, because they are orthogonal as patterns. So the
harmonic count is the room for occupants SPREAD over the sphere, which
are told apart by their pattern and never by their place. The atom's
occupants are such flows: "count the windings, flow the phases"; spin is
the turning along the Hopf fibre through EVERY point.

**What a body is.** A body is a cap about its place, half-angle 180·a
degrees, where a is its agitation (§2, accepted). Two bodies meet where
their caps meet (the encounter law), so the room for occupants LOCALISED in
caps is counted by places that do not meet:

- each completely orthogonal plane's circle, since any point of one such
  circle stands exactly 90° from every point of another (orthogonal planes
  share no direction, so their unit vectors are at right angles); and
- along one circle, as many same-sense bodies as their caps keep apart.
  Bodies of one shell and one sense turn at one rate (`δ` at one reach), so
  they keep their spacing and never meet. Counter-sensed bodies meet twice
  a turn.

**The threshold.** A body's places stay apart only while its cap stays
within a hemisphere: two caps of half-angle 90° or more always meet,
wherever they stand, so no arrangement of places can separate them, and
only orthogonal patterns can. A half-angle of 90° is `180·a = 90`, so
**a = ½**. That is the threshold `formation::ignition_share` already rules:
"None at a ≥ ½: light pushes back before the heart forms". Matter at
a < ½ can gather into a heart and stand as a localised body; matter at
a ≥ ½ cannot, and stays a flow.

**So, one grammar in two regimes:**

| The occupant | Its cap | Counted by | Room in a shell |
|---|---|---|---|
| a body (a < ½: star, world, moon) | within a hemisphere | places that do not meet: completely orthogonal planes, and same-sense spacing along each | read from the bodies' caps (§5 reading 1, refined: a plane holds as many as their caps keep apart, not one) |
| a flow (a ≥ ½, or spread by its own law: the atom's windings) | the whole sphere | orthogonal patterns: the degree-l harmonics | the harmonic count (reading 2) |

Coupled tiers: heating widens a cap, and past ½ a body dissolves into a
flow counted by patterns. Cooling narrows it, and below ½ a flow can
gather into a body counted by places. The atom's windings are flows by
their own mechanism. Stars and worlds are bodies by theirs.

**What changes in §5 and §6:** bodies of a shell share a plane whenever
their caps keep them apart along it, so a shell's room for bodies is read
from their caps and never capped at the plane count. Bodies in different
completely orthogonal planes still never meet. The tension in §6 dissolves:
the harmonic count and the plane count are one grammar's two limits, with
the cap at a = ½ between them.

### 6b. Corrections to 6a (with xylarium)

The a = ½ threshold holds under any measure: half the volume of any S^n is
a hemisphere, by symmetry, and along a circle two arcs longer than half a
turn always overlap. Three corrections to 6a stand:

1. **Closeness must be two-sided (owed).** §2's `C = Σ w_k·in_step(Δ_k)`
   uses only the centre body's weights. Between two bodies the sphere's own
   closeness takes both: `cos d = Σ_k √(w_k·w′_k)·cos Δ_k`. For a body
   turning wholly in plane j and another wholly in plane k ≠ j, every term
   vanishes, so d = 90°: the "90° apart everywhere" of 6a, read correctly.
   The one-sided C leaves Δ_k undefined in planes where the centre has no
   weight. The ruled linear measure has no replacement yet for the
   root `√(w·w′)`. It has the same root as the corners xylarium found where
   weights split. Owed before any room on S^n is counted.
2. **Same-sense bodies overtake.** `δ = H/(R + H)` varies across a shell's
   band and across a body's own spread `R(1 ± a)`, so same-sense bodies of
   one shell do not keep their spacing: they overtake and meet (§5). The
   room for same-sense bodies is not a static count. The encounter law
   reads it over time, and only lock-step (equal δ) holds a spacing.
3. **A single-plane body is an arc along its circle**, of 360·a degrees,
   not a cap on S^n. By volume, a cap with share a < ½ on S^360 has an
   angular radius near 90° for any a that is not tiny, so sphere caps would
   leave no room. The cap on S^n is the reading for split weights.

6a's result stands with these: one grammar in two regimes, read from the
occupant's own round. Bodies (a < ½) are counted by places that do not
meet, read over time by the encounter law. Flows (a ≥ ½, and the atom's
windings) are counted by the harmonics.

### 6c. The two-sided closeness in tangents (xylarium's 11.0 (27) R6; SUPERSEDED for netstratum by §6d, in degrees)

A body's share of its turning across its P planes is a direction: a point
x on the positive part of S^(P−1). It is carried as rational half-angle
tangents, so its amplitudes x_k are exact rationals with `Σ x_k² = 1` by
construction (inverse stereographic projection, as xylarium's rotor
`Half`), and the plane weights are `w_k = x_k²`. The sphere's closeness
between two bodies, `cos d = Σ √(w_k·w′_k)·cos Δ_k`, becomes
`Σ x_k·x′_k·cos Δ_k`: no root is taken. With the ruled linear measure's
signed form in place of the cosine, `cos Δ → 2·in_step(Δ) − 1 = 1 − 4|Δ|`:

    κ = Σ_k x_k·x′_k·(1 − 4|Δ_k|),    C₂ = (1 + κ)/2

Checks:

- a body with itself: C₂ = 1;
- one plane, both wholly in it: C₂ = 1 − 2|Δ| = in_step(Δ), so docs/79's
  coherence is the one-plane case;
- bodies in completely orthogonal planes: κ = 0 and C₂ = ½, which is 90°;
- antiphase in one plane: C₂ = 0;
- a single-plane body against a point on another plane's circle: C₂ = ½, so
  its round is the arc of 6b and never a wedge.

C₂ replaces §2's one-sided C. A body's cap is `C₂ ≥ 1 − a`, and plane
weights are held as half-angle tangents, never as shares.

**Two notes for the operators:**

- **The corners.** Contours of C₂ are piecewise linear: each plane's
  `|Δ_k|` bends at Δ_k = 0 and ½. That corner is the ruled linear measure's
  own (docs/79's `1 − 2|Δ|` bends there on one circle), so on netstratum's
  reading it is already ruled. xylarium routes it to the operators (its
  11.4: corners are intelligence's).
- **The square in the carrier.** The amplitudes are formed from the
  tangents by `x = 2t/(1 + |t|²)`, which squares a parameter in the
  arithmetic, though no law reads a squared physical quantity. Whether
  "laws as angles" admits that conversion is the operators' to say.

## 7. The map between tiers (first sketch; SUPERSEDED by §7′, which finds its dilation wrong)

**From the enclosure law** (`enclosure`): each galaxy's S^360 and each
star system's S^60 is "hung inside the sphere above at its core: the core
is the THROAT joining the two". Each enclosure is held in ITS OWN UNITS:
its rim is its own holding, so in its own units it spans
`[throat 1, rim 2]`. Each tier counts its turns on its own ring.

**So the map from a child enclosure to its parent's sphere is three exact
pieces, each a reading already held:**

1. **Where:** the child's throat stands at its anchor's place on the
   parent's sphere, its polar share and its phase in each plane (§1 and §2:
   exact angles). Placing the child's frame there is a TURNING by those
   angles, composed plane by plane.
2. **How large:** the child's unit is its own holding, so one unit of the
   child is `H_child / H_parent` units of the parent. That ratio is the
   DILATION, an exact rational and never a constant.
3. **Which planes:** the child's planes are its own sphere's. By the layer
   ruling they stand orthogonal to the anchor's own plane through the
   throat (§5, the vertical axis of the fill). The anchor's turning carries
   the child's whole frame rigidly (xylarium 11.3b: a parent's residue is a
   rigid turn of its children's sphere).

The map is the versor `turning(anchor's place) · dilation(H_child/H_parent)`.
A route between two bodies (`enclosure::route`) composes these maps up
through each throat to the enclosure they share and down again. A reading
across tiers (§3) carries the body's cap through the route: the dilations
scale its reach spread, and the turnings place its angles. Every piece is
rational and nothing is squared: angles add per plane (§1), and the
dilation is a ratio of holdings.

**Owed to the build, not to the derivation:** the throat's own crossing
(puncture/seal, §4), where a body passes from one sphere's frame to the
other's rather than being read across.

**Refinement (xylarium):** the dilation acts on HALF-ANGLES, never on
degrees. A conformal dilation of the sphere about the throat multiplies
every direction's half-angle tangent by one factor, `t → λ·t`, with
`λ = H_child / H_parent`. So a cap crossing a tier changes its tangent
exactly and rationally, and its angle does not scale in proportion (a
uniform scaling of degrees is not conformal away from the throat). Reach
scales by λ as a length. The anchor's place stays in exact degrees per
plane (§1). Its rotor is formed only for a reading, at the observer's depth.

## 7′. The conformal map per tier, derived in tangents (operator 2026-10-20: "derive the conformal map per tier"; steps 3–6 SUPERSEDED for netstratum by §7″, in degrees)

Derived step by step from built and ruled laws. It corrects §7 and the
refinement under it in two places, marked.

**Step 1: what of the child is mapped.** The enclosure law holds a child
enclosure in its own units on `[throat 1, rim 2]`, and a place's polar
angle is its reach over the rim, a share of a half turn
(`enclosure::place`). So the throat stands at 90° from the child's core
and the rim at 180°, its antipode. The child's content is a HEMISPHERE
of its own sphere: the far half, centred on its rim point and bounded by
its throat circle. The near half lies behind the throat. Both edges read
the same deficit, δ = ½ (the rim law: at the throat H = 1, R = 1; at the
rim H = 2, R = 2).

**Step 2: what of the parent receives it.** On the parent's sphere the
child holds its gravity's reach about its place: the pinch
`d = R·H_c/(H_c + H_p)` (operator 2026-10-18, built in the capture
`breath.rs`). It is a CAP about the child's place.

**Step 3: the map (corrects §7).** Conformal maps of a sphere (its Möbius
maps) take caps to caps and the whole sphere to the whole sphere, and
nothing else. A whole child sphere can never be laid conformally on a cap
of the parent, so §7's `t → λt` applied to the whole sphere, and "a
dilation blows a cap up into a whole sphere", cannot be conformal. A
HEMISPHERE can: in half-angle tangents about each centre, `u ∈ [0, 1]`
on the child's hemisphere and `t ∈ [0, τ]` on the parent's cap, the map is

    t = τ · u,   then the turning that places the cap at the child's place

with τ the parent cap's half-angle tangent. It is exact and rational in
tangents, and composes as Möbius maps do.

**Step 4: the factor (corrects §7's λ).** The conformal factor is τ, the
size of the child's region on the parent, read from gravity's reach. It is
not the hold ratio `H_child/H_parent`. The hold ratio converts the child's
unit of reach into the parent's (a length), and it enters τ only through
d.

**Step 5: the orientation is forced.** The hemisphere's centre is the
child's RIM point and its edge is the child's THROAT circle. So the child's
rim lands at the child's place on the parent (the cap's centre), and its
throat lands on the edge of its gravity's reach. Seen from the parent, a
child is turned inside out through its throat. This is the kind of map the
ruled inversion is (operator 2026-10-19: inversion through the throat, the
throat its fixed set). Whether it is that inversion exactly is **owed**.
Its fixed point R = H maps 2 to ½, not to 1, so they are not obviously the
same map.

**Step 6: the measure, a ruling owed.** τ is exact only if the parent's
places are carried as half-angle tangents. The enclosure law carries
places in linear degrees (polar share, phase in turns), and for a rational
arc the tangent `tan(180°·d/(2·rim))` is irrational (Niven). So the carrier
must choose which is exact:

- **Places in half-angle tangents**: every tier's map is exact and
  conformal, and composes along any route. The C₂ amplitudes (§6c) are

### 6d. The two-sided closeness, in degrees

**A body's split across its planes is an angle.** Over P planes, a body's
turning is a direction in the planes' orthant, given by tilt angles in
degrees: for two planes, one tilt χ, 0° wholly in the first and 90° wholly
in the second. The tilt is READ from the body's spin split by the linear
measure, `χ = 90°·ℓ_c/(ℓ_o + ℓ_c)` (the share as a quarter turn), never set.
More planes nest tilts the same way, one per further plane.

**The sphere's closeness, written in angles only.** Between two bodies the
sphere's own closeness is a sum of products of cosines and sines of their
tilts and phase offsets. By the product-to-sum identities that sum has ONE
exact form as a sum of single cosines of whole-number combinations of the
angles, with rational coefficients (its Fourier form). For two planes:

    cos d = ¼[cos(χ−χ′−Δ₁) + cos(χ−χ′+Δ₁) + cos(χ−χ′−Δ₂) + cos(χ−χ′+Δ₂)]
          + ¼[cos(χ+χ′−Δ₁) + cos(χ+χ′+Δ₁) − cos(χ+χ′−Δ₂) − cos(χ+χ′+Δ₂)]

**The derived form: read each cosine by the ruled measure.**

    κ = the same sum with every cos(θ) read as L(θ),    C = (1 + κ)/2

Every angle in it is an exact rational number of degrees, and every
coefficient an exact rational.

Checks, each worked exactly:

- **itself** (χ = χ′, Δ = 0): every first-bracket term is L(0) = 1, and the
  second bracket cancels pairwise, so κ = 1 and C = 1;
- **both wholly in one plane** (χ = χ′ = 0): κ = L(Δ₁), so
  `C = 1 − |Δ₁|/180° = in_step(Δ₁)`: docs/79's coherence is the one-plane
  case;
- **wholly in completely orthogonal planes** (χ = 0, χ′ = 90°): every pair
  `L(90° + y) + L(90° − y) = 0`, so κ = 0 and C = ½, which is 90°;
- **antiphase in one plane** (Δ₁ = 180°): κ = −1 and C = 0;
- **a single-plane body against a point on another plane's circle**:
  C = ½, so its round is the arc of §6b, never a wedge.

The body's cap is `C ≥ 1 − a` (§2). This replaces both §2's one-sided C and
§6c's tangent form for netstratum.

**Owed:** the Fourier form's terms multiply with the planes a body has
weight in. Most bodies turn in few planes, and every term of a plane at
tilt 0° or 90° collapses, but the general form wants a peel (no sweep over

### 7‴. Keeping the geometry non-Euclidean (operator 2026-10-20: "we want to keep this non-euclidean geometry")

**The flaw in §7″.** It justified the dilation by "a circle's length is
linear in its radius", which is the flat, Euclidean relation. On a sphere a
circle about a point grows to the equator at 90° and shrinks back to
nothing at the antipode at 180°. That return is what makes the space closed
and non-Euclidean, and §7″'s argument dropped it.

**The sphere's circles in the ruled measure.** The sphere's own law is that
a circle's length goes as the SINE of its radius. Read in the ruled linear
measure, the sine is `S(ψ) = L(90° − ψ) = 1 − |90° − ψ|/90°`: 0 at the
point, 1 at the equator, and 0 again at the antipode. That is the sphere's
closing, held in degrees with no trigonometry.

**The map that keeps shapes in that measure.** A map ψ ↦ φ from the
child's hemisphere keeps shapes when it stretches across the radius as it
stretches along it: `dφ/dψ = S(φ)/S(ψ)`. Solved exactly in each half of S,
it gives:

- **while the cap stays within its hemisphere** (α ≤ 90°): φ = k·ψ with
  `k = α/90°`, the uniform scaling of §7″, now justified by the sphere's
  own S, since S(ψ) = ψ/90° below the equator;
- **when the cap passes the equator** (α > 90°): φ = k·ψ up to
  `ψ₀ = 90°/k`, then `φ = 180° − 8100/(k·ψ)` (degrees²) beyond it, with
  `k = 90°/(180° − α)`. The two pieces meet at the equator with the same
  slope, and the child's throat circle (ψ = 90°) lands exactly on α. Beyond
  the equator the circles shrink again, and the map follows them.

Every piece is an exact rational of degrees. Along a route the pieces
compose exactly.

**What the ruled measure makes of the sphere** (for the operators to know,
not a new ruling). The linear measure bends only where L bends: at the
point, the equator and the antipode. A sphere read in it is two flat caps
joined at the equator, with all of its curvature carried on the equator
and its total that of the sphere. It is closed, finite, and
non-Euclidean as a whole: circles return to nothing, and a triangle that
crosses the equator has angles summing past 180°. Within either half,
lengths read as in the flat. This is the same concentration that gives
in_step its corners at 0 and ½ turn (§6c's corners).

**A flat relation found in a built law.** The crest collapse
(`formation::crest_collapse`) reads a crest ring at reach R as having length
R ("one turn, no π"). That is the flat relation. In the sphere's measure a
crest ring's length is `S` of its polar angle (its reach over the rim, in
half turns), which shrinks again past the equator. It is owed a correction
with the cut-over.
